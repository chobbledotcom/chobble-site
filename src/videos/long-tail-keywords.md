# "Long tail" keywords

Trying to rank for "pizza" or "plumber"? Good luck competing with Pizza Hut and billion-pound brands. This video explains why longer, more specific search phrases are your secret weapon - stuff like "vegan pizza delivery in Prestwich" where local businesses can compete.

**Want to understand keyword strategy in more depth? [Click here to read my comprehensive guide (10-minute read)](/guides/keywords-and-keyword-stuffing/).**

When someone searches "solicitor" they could want anything, but "no win no fee cycling accident solicitor Manchester" - that's someone who knows exactly what they need. I'll show you how to find these long tail keywords for your business and why they're really useful for getting actual customers, not just random browsers. It takes the fight away from the big boys and lets you compete where you've got a chance.

In the video I search for "pizza" on [Startpage.com](https://www.startpage.com/), which queries Google anonymously so you see what a stranger sees rather than results personalised around where Google knows I am - and the first page is all the big international names: Pizza Hut, Domino's, Pizza Express, the Wikipedia page. Then I search "vegan pizza in Manchester" and every entirely vegan pizza place in the city turns up on page one, because there's only a handful of them and they're exactly what that searcher is after. Same city, same pizza, completely different fight.

Searcher intent is the other half of it: "what are vegan pizza toppings" wants information, "Pureza opening times" wants to visit, and someone typing a long, specific phrase is usually close to a decision. If you've got accreditations or qualifications that mark you out as good at what you do, the video covers why it's worth hammering those home - the ideal visitor knows exactly what they want, and long-tail searchers are the most likely to be that kind of visitor. It's the keyword strategy behind the client sites in my [examples](/examples/), a fair few of which rank locally.
