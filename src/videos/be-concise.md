# Be concise

Most business websites have a skewed "waffle to useful content" ratio - especially in the age of AI, which makes it really easy to generate paragraphs of text. This video uses a fictional example from a building company to explain how you can use less words to convey more information and make your site more valuable to its visitors.

## The waffle demo

To make the point properly I wrote a deliberately waffly page for a fictional Manchester loft conversion company on the [Chobble Template](/services/chobble-template/) demo site, and then read it out loud in the video. It sounds professional the whole way through and says nothing at all - nowhere in all those words is there a single specific about the actual business. This is the stuff I see all the time on real sites, and it usually got there one of two ways: copied from somebody else's website, or generated with AI and put up unedited.

## The concise fix

It'll show you how to replace 200 words of AI-generated fluff with 20 words that matter - information that visitors want. Every page really has one job: answering why a visitor should choose your business over someone else for that specific product or service, and waffle buries the answer. In the video I compare the waffle page against a concise rewrite of the same product, where the description is a fraction of the length but the features and specifications lists break the specifics out into scannable bits, the gallery has pictures people actually click on, and the meta content is filled in properly. Fewer words, easier to understand, and much more likely to result in a sale.

It takes 10 minutes to watch, and it's free - no tools to buy, just a way of thinking about the words on your site. The sites in my [examples](/examples/) are mostly built the concise way, if you want to see the results.
