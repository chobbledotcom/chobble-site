# Targeting local areas on Google SERPs: a real-ish example

Missing out on customers from nearby towns because they can't find you when they search locally? This video walks through creating location pages using a real example - a Prestwich gym targeting folk in Whitefield. It shows you exactly how to research and write pages that work.

**Rather read about this in detail? [Click here to read my guide (8-minute read)](/guides/targeting-specific-areas/).**

I've got 9 questions you need to answer about each area you want to target - stuff like transport links, local competition, what makes you different. Then I show you how to turn that research into a page that's genuinely useful, not just "we also serve X area" rubbish. One good location page a month beats ten copy-paste jobs. It works for any business where people will travel a bit for your service.

The video works through all nine - how will residents get to my business, what similar local businesses exist, what makes mine different, do I have testimonials from the area, what are the logistical quirks - for a fictional Prestwich fitness studio targeting Whitefield: a couple of stops on the Metrolink, the gyms you'd be up against there, what a beginners-focused studio does differently from the big brands. Copy-pasted location pages do sort of work, I've seen it, but the ones that do exceptionally well are the ones that think about the specific nuances of delivering that service in that area. The [Fun at the Fair example](/examples/fun-at-the-fair/) is the real version of that demo - 21 location pages built exactly this way.
