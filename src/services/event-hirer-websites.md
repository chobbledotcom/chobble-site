# Websites for event hirers in Manchester

If you hire out equipment, staging, marquees, games, PA, lighting, furniture or anything else that goes on a van and comes back, your website has two jobs: show people what you've got, and take the booking without creating more admin for the office. Those are different jobs, and plenty of hire company sites are good at one and hopeless at the other.

I build both parts. The website side is a fast, mobile-friendly catalogue built on my [Chobble Template](/services/chobble-template/), with product pages, specifications, pricing, location pages and reviews. The booking side is [Chobble Tickets](https://tickets.chobble.com), the open source booking system I built, which handles per-date stock, multi-day hire, deposits, delivery and collection runs. The two can work together or the booking system can run as the whole site, depending on how much catalogue you need.

## The website part

Hire websites live or die on the catalogue. Somebody planning an event wants to know what you've got, how big it is, whether it will fit, what it costs for the dates they need, and whether you deliver to their postcode. If they have to ring you to find any of that out, plenty of them won't bother.

So the product pages carry the boring facts that win the booking: dimensions, power requirements, setup time, access needs, what's included, what it costs for one day or a week, and the delivery area. Fun Pro UK's product pages do this across 88 products, with tiered pricing from one-day to seven-day hire and the space and power specs on each one. Fun at the Fair does the same for fairground rides, where the footprint and height matter before anybody gets a van out.

Then there's the quoting. A corporate event might want six games, a photo booth and a branded panel, and the last thing the office needs is a phone call that turns into a manual quote. Fun Pro UK has a quote basket that lets a visitor build the whole order in the browser and submit it with the event details, which means the enquiry arrives with the products already chosen.

The rest is local search and proof. Location pages for the towns and cities you cover, Google reviews pulled onto the site, and a fast mobile experience, because a lot of this browsing happens on a phone in the evening.

## The booking part

I spent ten years as lead developer at Bouncy Castle Network, a booking platform that grew from its first customer to over a thousand hire companies, so I know what actually goes wrong when you put hire stock online. I also spent five years as a senior developer at Bandcamp, which taught me a lot about performance under load and building things that people trust with their own data. Both of those jobs fed into [Chobble Tickets](https://tickets.chobble.com), the booking system I built myself.

It does the things hire companies need rather than the things a ticket marketplace wants:

- **Stock by date.** Each listing has a capacity per day, so a three-day hire uses the stock on all three days, and the system won't sell the same item twice. You can block holidays and maintenance without creating a fake booking.
- **Duration pricing.** You set a price for each length of hire, such as one day at £50 or three days at £120, and the customer chooses from the durations you offer.
- **Deposits and balances.** Take a percentage or fixed deposit at booking and send the customer a secure link to pay the rest later. Cash and bank transfers can be recorded too.
- **Delivery and collection.** Each booking gets a drop-off and a collection, assigned to a driver, van or crew. Drivers sign in to a run sheet of their own jobs, with the address and phone number, and mark each leg done as they go.
- **Payment straight to you.** Customers pay through your own Stripe, Square or SumUp account. Chobble takes no percentage of the booking.
- **Your data.** It's open source, the database is encrypted, and you can export your listings, bookings and reports. Nothing is held hostage if you leave.

The software is £50 a year or £5 a month for managed hosting, with every feature included. If you'd rather I set it up, import your catalogue and stay on hand for questions, that's £40 a month with no setup fee. The [tickets site](https://tickets.chobble.com) has the full detail, but you don't need to think of it as a separate product - on this page it's the booking engine behind your website.

The first is a website with the booking system behind it. I build the public site on the Chobble Template, with the product catalogue, specs, location pages and reviews, then embed the booking pages or use the order widget so customers can add items to a cart from your existing site and finish checkout in the booking system. That's the shape for a company with a big catalogue and a brand to protect.

The second is to run the whole site on Chobble Tickets. It includes a public listings page, content pages and the booking flow, so if your website mostly exists to show stock and take bookings, there's no reason to build two things. It won't give you a completely bespoke design, but it will get you online quickly and the booking side is already done.

I'll tell you which one fits once I've seen what you hire and how you sell it. There's no point building a custom catalogue if the booking system's own pages would do the job, and there's no point squeezing a 90-product hire range into a template that can't show it properly.
